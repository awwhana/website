01 / Company Overview

From a cotton trading house to a listed fashion powerhouse.

ABLBL's story is inseparable from the Aditya Birla Group's 165-year manufacturing lineage and the more recent restructuring of its fashion business.

Corporate lineage

1857

Aditya Birla Group founded

Seth Shiv Narayan Birla starts a cotton trading business in Pilani, Rajasthan, the root of a group now spanning 41 countries.

Legacy era

Madura Garments established

One of India's pioneering apparel manufacturers; later evolves into Madura Fashion & Lifestyle (MFL) under the Group.

2015

Aditya Birla Fashion & Retail (ABFRL) formed

MFL integrates with Pantaloons Fashion & Retail and other apparel businesses, creating one of India's largest fashion companies.

May 2025

ABLBL demerged from ABFRL

Madura Fashion & Lifestyle is spun off as an independent listed entity; ABFRL shareholders receive ABLBL shares 1:1. ABLBL becomes the dedicated manufacturing, sourcing and wholesale arm for the Group's marquee lifestyle brands, while ABFRL retains Pantaloons, ethnic wear and value retail.

Jun 2025

Listed on NSE / BSE

ABLBL debuts on the NSE at ₹167/share, commanding a market cap of roughly ₹20,500 crore at listing.

02 / ABLBL Business Model

Demand led, centrally planned manufacturing.

Production at ABLBL is not push-manufacturing against a forecast, it is pulled from confirmed advance orders, then executed through a disciplined Cut-Make system.

The planning cycle

Step 1

Trade Shows (AW & SS)

Held twice yearly for Autumn/Winter and Spring/Summer seasons. Buyers across every sales channel view collections and place advance orders.

Step 2

Brand Commercial Demand Sheet (BCDS)

Orders are consolidated into the BCDS, the master document that drives sourcing, production planning and manufacturing.

Step 3

Cross-functional execution

Merchandising, the Technical Management Cell (TMC), Planning, Industrial Engineering, CAD, Stores and Production convert design concepts into finished garments against the BCDS.

Step 4

Central coordination

The Madura Fashion & Lifestyle (MFL) Head Office centrally allocates production targets, budgets and demand across all 11 units for optimum capacity utilisation.

Cut Make (CM) model

ABLBL manufactures on a Cut Make basis, with operational efficiency tracked through four core levers:

CPM
Cost Per Minute, total cost of running the line per minute of production.
SMV
Standard Minute Value, the benchmark time to produce one garment operation.
Productivity
Output achieved against the SMV benchmark, line by line.
Line efficiency
How closely actual line performance tracks planned capacity.
Unit economics · EGL

₹5.86 /minute

Approximate Cost Per Minute at the Europa Garments Ltd. (EGL) unit, covering labour, machinery, utilities, maintenance and overheads. Production and budgeting are planned to maximise capacity utilisation while protecting this margin.

04 / Consumer & Market Study

A premiumising, digitally led apparel market.

ABLBL's brands sit inside India's branded menswear led apparel market, a category being reshaped by rising incomes, tier-2/3 expansion and omnichannel retail.

Market forces at play

Premiumisation
Urban millennials & Gen Z increasingly trade up from unorganised to branded, design led apparel, the core growth lever cited for ABLBL's lifestyle brands.
Tier 2/3 expansion
Organised retail penetration is deepening beyond metros, supporting EBO and MBO store additions across 785+ towns and cities.
Omnichannel shift
Ecommerce (B2B and B2C) is now a standing sales channel alongside EBOs, MBOs and department stores, not a bolt-on.
Occasion & workwear normalisation
Hybrid work has blurred formal/casual lines, a dynamic Van Heusen and Allen Solly are both built to exploit from opposite ends.

06 / Distribution & Channel Strategy

Six ways ABLBL's garments reach a customer.

A diversified network built to respond to demand across geographies and buying occasions — from owned stores to export.

3,300+

Exclusive Brand Outlets (EBOs), run under FOFO (Franchise Owned Franchise Operated) and FOSO (Franchise Owned Store Operated) models.

37,500+

Multi-Brand Outlets (MBOs) and organised trade partners carrying ABLBL brands.

7,000+

Shop-in-shops inside department stores and organised retail chains.

Channel mix

1. EBO
Brand-controlled retail environment; primary driver of full-price sell-through and brand experience.
2. MBO
Wide-reach wholesale distribution into multi-brand retail across smaller towns.
3. Department store shop-in-shops
7,000+ dedicated brand counters inside department stores and organised retail chains.
4. Ecommerce — B2C
Own D2C site plus marketplace presence (e.g. Myntra, Amazon, Flipkart-type platforms).
5. Ecommerce — B2B
Digital bulk/online wholesale ordering for MBO and department store partners.
6. Export
EGL and other units supply export customers, including Dubai and Mauritius, when capacity exceeds domestic demand.

Ecommerce study — why it matters

Branded apparel in India has been steadily shifting online, with fashion marketplaces and brand D2C sites now standard parts of the retail calendar (End of Season Sale, festive drops). For ABLBL this means:

  • Online acts as a discovery and full price channel for younger, Allen Solly / Van Heusen leaning shoppers.
  • B2B ecommerce lets department store and MBO partners reorder digitally, tightening the BCDS to shelf loop.
  • Omnichannel inventory visibility is now a working capital lever, not just a customer convenience.

Where ABLBL sits in India's branded apparel race.

Formal-to-casual menswear is ABLBL's core battleground, contested by heritage textile houses, multi-brand licence holders and global fast fashion.

ABLBL's relative edge

  • Only major player owning both manufacturing depth (11 integrated units) and a four brand formal to casual ladder under one demand planning system.
  • Reebok licence gives category reach into performance sportswear that pure menswear formal rivals (Raymond, Arvind) lack at scale.
  • Aditya Birla Group backing on capital, sourcing and real estate negotiation for EBO expansion.

Watch-outs

  • Raymond Lifestyle's heritage pricing power in premium suiting/occasion wear.
  • Value-fashion majors (Zudio, Style Up) compressing entry-price formalwear margins.
  • D2C native challenger brands (e.g. The Formal Club) undercutting on price via digital first models.
